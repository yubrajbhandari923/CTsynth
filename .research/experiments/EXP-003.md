Reporting s1 alone on a two-regime phantom would rank a clipped estimator above an exact one (F-016). This is a benchmark-design result that applies to the real AAPM data too, where boundary pixels are ~5% of the image.
- **Limitations:** SYNTHETIC DATA ONLY - no AAPM case data was processed, so no AAPM-dependent or leaderboard-comparable number exists. 32x32 vs the real 512x512; the official s1 divides by 3*512^2 so the magnitudes are not comparable. Image-domain only (no fan-beam projector, so the transmission-domain methods the winners used are untested). TV lam=1e-4 was picked by a coarse scan on the same synthetic problem, so its 36% gain is optimistic. AIR_CUTOFF_PER_CM=0.1 is a modelling choice untested on real data. No U-Net yet (T-012).
- **Unresolved:** Whether the boundary pathology has the same magnitude on real AAPM data (boundary fraction and PSF differ). ## Findings produced

- **F-016** On SYNTHETIC AAPM-schema data (EXP-003/RUN-0003, 17 val cases, 32x32, noiseless), an exact-on-tissue estimator is WORSE THAN PREDICTING NOTHING by the official aggregate metric. classical_sum_to_one recovers tissue pixels to RMSE 1.29e-6 (near machine precision, as theory predicts for noiseless data) yet scores s1 = 1.179, far worse than the no-training zero_prediction reference (s1 = 0.430), because the 5-9% boundary band carries RMSE 3.84 with individual values up to 11.5 volume fraction (grossly unphysical). Simplex projection clips those outliers and wins (s1 = 0.0508) WITHOUT improving the model: its tissue RMSE is the same 1.2e-6. CONSEQUENCE: a pooled RMSE over a two-regime phantom is dominated by the smallest, most mis-modelled population, so reporting s1 alone would rank a clipped estimator above an exact one for the wrong reason. Regime-separated reporting is mandatory for this benchmark. Caveat: synthetic data only; boundary-band fraction and geometry differ from real AAPM data. (preliminary)
