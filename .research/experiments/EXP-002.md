# EXP-002 · Spectral identifiability toy: bins x basis size x spectrum mismatch

## Hypothesis

Pre-registered. Per-ray identifiability is set by rank of the count Jacobian wrt line integrals A (n_meas x k). Predictions: (a) 1 measurement, k>=2: rank 1, not identifiable; (b) 2 measurements (80/120 kVp), k=2: identifiable, finite condition number; (c) 2 measurements, k=3: rank<=2<3, NOT identifiable, no recovery claim; (d) 4 photon-counting bins on one 120 kVp exposure, k=3: identifiable but with larger noise amplification than dual-kVp k=2; (e) 2-material model on a 3-material phantom is biased (misspecification) and the bias does not shrink as counts increase; (f) nominal-spectrum estimator on perturbed-spectrum data is biased, bias independent of photon count; (g) empirical Monte-Carlo std of A-hat matches the Cramer-Rao bound sqrt(diag((J^T W J)^-1)) within ~15% at high counts (validates the whole stack).

## Motivation

First use of ctsynth.physics; tests which measurement designs make material inference well posed before any learning method (D-B, H2).

## Method

2D 48x48 phantom, pixel 0.5 cm, parallel beam 90 views x 72 dets. Materials water/iodine/calcium as independent partial mass densities (g/cm^3), idealised additive mixture (not volume conserving). Phantoms: P2 (water+iodine inserts 5,10,20 mg/cm^3), P3 (adds calcium inserts 0.1,0.3 g/cm^3). Designs at EQUAL total unattenuated photons per ray N_tot in {1e5,1e6}: M1 one 120 kVp energy-integrating bin; M2 80 and 120 kVp each N_tot/2; M4 four photon-counting windows [20-50,50-70,70-90,90-120] keV on one 120 kVp exposure. Estimator: per-ray Poisson MLE (Levenberg-Marquardt) of A with k in {2,3}, then unconstrained linear least squares for rho from A-hat; no positivity or other priors. Mismatch: estimator uses nominal spectra; data generated with spectrum tilt +-10% and energy scale +-2%. 20 noise realisations per cell, fixed seeds via ctsynth.seeding.rng. Metrics: Jacobian singular values and condition number, CRLB noise amplification, empirical std vs CRLB, per-material insert RMSE and bias (g/cm^3), written to metrics JSON. Cells with rank<k are reported as non-identifiable, not scored.

## Findings produced

- **F-001** Material identifiability in this toy spectral CT model is set by the rank of the Poisson-weighted count Jacobian, not by the estimator: one energy-integrating measurement gives rank 1 on every sampled ray (cannot separate 2+ materials), dual-kVp gives rank 2 (separates 2 materials, median condition number 195), and dual-kVp cannot separate 3 materials (rank 2 < 3). Four photon-counting bins separate 3 materials but with 2.70x the iodine CRLB noise of dual-kVp 2-material at equal total photons. (superseded)
- **F-002** The forward model plus per-ray Poisson MLE is statistically well-calibrated: empirical Monte-Carlo standard deviation of estimated line integrals matches the Cramer-Rao bound within 11% for every identifiable cell at 1e6 photons (0.87-1.11 across water/iodine/calcium), with zero LM convergence failures. This validates the forward model, Jacobian and estimator as a consistent stack. (superseded)
- **F-003** Fitting a 2-material basis to 3-material data produces bias that does NOT shrink with photon count (bias ratio 1e6/1e5 = 0.89 and 1.02 versus 0.32 expected for noise-driven error), at 7-15x the noise floor: basis misspecification is a systematic error that more dose cannot fix. (preliminary)
- **F-004** PARTIAL FALSIFICATION of pre-registered prediction (f): a nominal-spectrum estimator on mismatched-spectrum data is NOT detectably biased in every design. 7 of 12 cells passed. All four dual-kVp cells on the 2-material phantom failed (bias only 0.73-1.77x its noise floor), as did one 4-bin cell. Photon-counting designs showed clear mismatch bias (6-8x floor) while dual-kVp did not. Interpretation (preliminary, needs a dedicated experiment): at +-10% tilt and +-2% energy scale the dual-kVp mismatch bias is at or below the detection floor of 20 realisations, so (f) was under-powered rather than physically wrong; an alternative explanation is that 2-material dual-kVp genuinely absorbs smooth spectral error into the basis coefficients. These are NOT distinguished by this experiment. (superseded)
- **F-005** SUPERSEDES F-004. Spectral mismatch always produces genuine bias; the apparent 'no bias' cells were a detection-power and scope artifact of the pre-registered criterion. Noise-free calculation (MLE on EXACT expected counts from perturbed spectra, zero Poisson noise, 20cm water + 0.2 g/cm2 iodine): every condition gives nonzero bias. Relative to the 20-realisation detection floor (CRLB/sqrt(20)) at 1e6 photons: dual-kVp iodine tilt +-10% = 0.93-1.05x (genuinely at the floor: UNDER-POWERED, not unbiased), dual-kVp iodine energy-scale +-2% = 4.0-4.2x (CLEARLY BIASED: the original F-004 wording was wrong for this case), dual-kVp WATER tilt = 3.8-4.1x (the iodine-only criterion hid a large water bias), 4-bin photon counting = 9-21x on the 2-material phantom. Two defects in the pre-registered criterion: it scored iodine only, and it required 3x the floor at BOTH dose levels so the noisier 1e5 level dominated the verdict. (superseded)
- **F-006** SUPERSEDES F-002. Empirical Monte-Carlo std agrees with the Cramer-Rao bound to within 12.5% (median over rays; range 0.875-1.106) for the three identifiable cells at 1e6 photons, with a systematic pattern (P2 cells low 0.87-0.95, P3 k=3 cells high 1.05-1.11) rather than scatter about 1. The sampling spread of this check is ~3-4% (median of per-ray std estimates over 26-54 rays, 20 draws each), so the deviation is real, not check noise. This is a SELF-CONSISTENCY check of Jacobian + sampler + MLE under one shared model and table: a wrong attenuation table or spectrum would pass it equally. It is not validation of physical fidelity. (supported)
- **F-007** SUPERSEDES F-001. Identifiability separates into a structural part and an empirical part. STRUCTURAL (forced by matrix shape, not an experimental result): with n_meas measurements and k unknown materials, rank <= min(n_meas,k), so 1 energy-integrating measurement cannot separate 2+ materials and dual-kVp cannot separate 3. EMPIRICAL (the real content): among designs that ARE identifiable, conditioning differs substantially and is dose-independent - dual-kVp 2-material median condition number 195 (max 506), 4-bin photon counting 2-material 99, 4-bin 3-material 217. Rank verdicts are robust: smallest/largest singular-value ratios are 2e-3 to 1e-2, so any threshold from 1e-2 to 1e-10 gives identical ranks. Noise cost at equal total photons, SAME basis size (k=2, iodine CRLB): 4-bin 0.0019 vs dual-kVp 0.0047 g/cm2, i.e. photon counting is 2.5x BETTER. The previously quoted 2.70x 'worse' figure compared M4 k=3 against M2 k=2 and confounded design with basis size; it is the price of estimating a third material, not a penalty of photon counting. (superseded)
- **F-008** AMENDS F-003 (scope). The dose-independent misspecification bias is an IODINE LINE-INTEGRAL statistic in g/cm2 (not g/cm3 density), measured on 27 rays of the 3-material phantom with a 2-material estimator. Both designs agree (bias ratio 1e6/1e5: M2 0.89, M4 1.02; 7.0x and 15.0x their noise floors). Dose-independence of misspecification bias is expected from theory, so this is a confirmation that the implementation behaves correctly, not a discovery. (supported)
- **F-009** SUPERSEDES F-005 (2nd independent review). Spectral mismatch ALWAYS biases a nominal-spectrum estimator, and on the real ray subset every condition is DETECTABLE with an appropriate test - no cell is genuinely 'unbiased'. Noise-free MLE on exact expected counts, 48 P2 rays, 1e6 photons, floor = rms(CRLB)/sqrt(20): dual-kVp iodine tilt +-10% gives rms/floor 0.82-0.92 BUT signed-mean z = +4.6/-5.2 (detectable); dual-kVp iodine energy-scale +-2% rms/floor 3.8-3.9, z = +-22; dual-kVp WATER tilt rms/floor 4.0-4.3, z = +-24 (a large bias the iodine-only criterion hid entirely); 4-bin photon counting on P2 is 21-38x floor. All fits converged. The pre-registered 3x-observed-rms criterion is DEFECTIVE: it compares a noise-inflated rms against an unsubtracted floor and so cannot distinguish 'no bias' from 'bias below the rms detection limit'. A signed-mean-over-rays test is the correct statistic. Supersedes the earlier 'under-powered, not unbiased' framing, which conceded too much to the defective criterion. (supported)
- **F-010** SUPERSEDES F-007 (2nd independent review). Identifiability: STRUCTURAL part (rank <= min(n_meas,k), so 1 measurement cannot separate 2+ materials and dual-kVp cannot separate 3) is arithmetic, not evidence, and holds at any threshold. EMPIRICAL part, on the real ray subset at 1e6 photons: rank verdicts are robust for rank thresholds 1e-3 to 1e-10 (100% full rank) but NOT at 1e-2, where the full-rank fraction collapses to 0% for dual-kVp k=2 and 4-bin k=3 and 58% for 4-bin k=2, because smallest/largest singular-value ratios are only 0.0020-0.0123. (The superseded F-007 wrongly claimed robustness up to 1e-2, contradicting the ratios quoted in its own sentence.) Median condition numbers: dual-kVp k=2 195 (max 506), 4-bin k=2 99, 4-bin k=3 217. At EQUAL basis size k=2 and equal total unattenuated photons, 4-bin photon counting has median iodine CRLB 0.00151 vs dual-kVp 0.00376 g/cm^2 = 2.5x better by median (2.4x by rms aggregation; the ratio is ray-dependent). This compares an idealised energy-weighted 2-exposure design against an idealised 4-window 1-exposure design with NO detector response, charge sharing or pile-up, so it is an upper bound on the photon-counting advantage. (supported)
