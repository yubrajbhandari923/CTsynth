# F-027 · SCOPE CORRECTION to F-015 and F-016 (reviewer3; Opus verified). Both were stated without the SYNTHETIC-ONLY qualifier they require, and they do not transfer. (a) F-015 said boundary pixels are the hard case; on REAL kit images the TISSUE pixels fail worst (sum_to_one tissue RMSE 1.823 vs all-pixel 1.555), because the dominant error is linear-model mismatch (F-025), not partition ambiguity. (b) F-016's ordering does not transfer: on real cases 0-2 the ranking is unconstrained 0.317 < simplex 0.379 < zero 0.447 << sum_to_one 1.555, so 'simplex wins' is FALSE on real data (it loses to the unconstrained estimator) while 'sum_to_one is worse than predicting nothing' HOLDS and is in fact far worse than on synthetic data. (c) The synthetic generator's boundary band is 9.5% of pixels versus 3.4-13.8% on real data depending on threshold, so the synthetic phantom is not a faithful stand-in for the boundary question. The surviving general claim from F-016 is the METRIC-DESIGN one: a pooled RMSE over a multi-regime phantom can rank a clipped estimator above an exact one, so regime-separated reporting is mandatory.

## Statement

SCOPE CORRECTION to F-015 and F-016 (reviewer3; Opus verified). Both were stated without the SYNTHETIC-ONLY qualifier they require, and they do not transfer. (a) F-015 said boundary pixels are the hard case; on REAL kit images the TISSUE pixels fail worst (sum_to_one tissue RMSE 1.823 vs all-pixel 1.555), because the dominant error is linear-model mismatch (F-025), not partition ambiguity. (b) F-016's ordering does not transfer: on real cases 0-2 the ranking is unconstrained 0.317 < simplex 0.379 < zero 0.447 << sum_to_one 1.555, so 'simplex wins' is FALSE on real data (it loses to the unconstrained estimator) while 'sum_to_one is worse than predicting nothing' HOLDS and is in fact far worse than on synthetic data. (c) The synthetic generator's boundary band is 9.5% of pixels versus 3.4-13.8% on real data depending on threshold, so the synthetic phantom is not a faithful stand-in for the boundary question. The surviving general claim from F-016 is the METRIC-DESIGN one: a pooled RMSE over a multi-regime phantom can rank a clipped estimator above an exact one, so regime-separated reporting is mandatory.
