# F-043 · IDENTIFIABILITY THEORY (deterministic, ideal-bin, per-ray model, calcification treated as unknown): DECT is rank 2 in 3 unknowns; with sum known precision is bounded; ideal PCCT gains ~2x only if calcification stays unknown

## Statement

Per ray, kit tables, equal INCIDENT photons (2e5), single ray, PCCT = bins of the 80 kVp beam only, ideal detector, no K-edge: 3-unknown Jacobian rank is structurally 1 (SECT), 2 (DECT 50/80, exact null direction); PCCT rank 3 is threshold-dependent (singular-value ratio 4e-5..1e-4, cutoff 1e-6), not structural. With the sum known (2 unknowns) DECT singular values are (3.457, 3.06e-3) at L=6cm 50/50. CRB std of adipose fraction per ray (calcification an unknown nuisance): DECT 0.249; PCCT-2/4/8/32 0.155/0.131/0.124/0.120; DECT/PCCT-32 ratio 1.9-2.2 over all 9 cases. If calcification is known absent and the sum known, DECT gives 0.025-0.041 (~10x better) and PCCT adds nothing; if calcification is absent but the sum unknown, DECT 0.20 vs PCCT 0.10. So 'additional spectral bins give at most ~2x' holds only inside the calcification-unknown model; the dominant lever is knowledge about calcification/sum, not spectral bins. The spectrum-weighted mu-curve angle 2.45-2.67 deg is a different quantity from F-012's 1.04 deg effective-matrix column angle.

## Limitations

Single-ray, no tomographic coupling or spatial prior; ideal detector; equal incident photons not equal dose; PCCT only 80 kVp spectrum; 3 path lengths x 3 compositions; metrics unbound; review R1 needs_work, corrected (the calcification-known cases were found by the reviewer).
