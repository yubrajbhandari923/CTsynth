# F-009 · SUPERSEDES F-005 (2nd independent review). Spectral mismatch ALWAYS biases a nominal-spectrum estimator, and on the real ray subset every condition is DETECTABLE with an appropriate test - no cell is genuinely 'unbiased'. Noise-free MLE on exact expected counts, 48 P2 rays, 1e6 photons, floor = rms(CRLB)/sqrt(20): dual-kVp iodine tilt +-10% gives rms/floor 0.82-0.92 BUT signed-mean z = +4.6/-5.2 (detectable); dual-kVp iodine energy-scale +-2% rms/floor 3.8-3.9, z = +-22; dual-kVp WATER tilt rms/floor 4.0-4.3, z = +-24 (a large bias the iodine-only criterion hid entirely); 4-bin photon counting on P2 is 21-38x floor. All fits converged. The pre-registered 3x-observed-rms criterion is DEFECTIVE: it compares a noise-inflated rms against an unsubtracted floor and so cannot distinguish 'no bias' from 'bias below the rms detection limit'. A signed-mean-over-rays test is the correct statistic. Supersedes the earlier 'under-powered, not unbiased' framing, which conceded too much to the defective criterion.

## Statement

SUPERSEDES F-005 (2nd independent review). Spectral mismatch ALWAYS biases a nominal-spectrum estimator, and on the real ray subset every condition is DETECTABLE with an appropriate test - no cell is genuinely 'unbiased'. Noise-free MLE on exact expected counts, 48 P2 rays, 1e6 photons, floor = rms(CRLB)/sqrt(20): dual-kVp iodine tilt +-10% gives rms/floor 0.82-0.92 BUT signed-mean z = +4.6/-5.2 (detectable); dual-kVp iodine energy-scale +-2% rms/floor 3.8-3.9, z = +-22; dual-kVp WATER tilt rms/floor 4.0-4.3, z = +-24 (a large bias the iodine-only criterion hid entirely); 4-bin photon counting on P2 is 21-38x floor. All fits converged. The pre-registered 3x-observed-rms criterion is DEFECTIVE: it compares a noise-inflated rms against an unsubtracted floor and so cannot distinguish 'no bias' from 'bias below the rms detection limit'. A signed-mean-over-rays test is the correct statistic. Supersedes the earlier 'under-powered, not unbiased' framing, which conceded too much to the defective criterion.
