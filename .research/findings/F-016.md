# F-016 · On SYNTHETIC AAPM-schema data (EXP-003/RUN-0003, 17 val cases, 32x32, noiseless), an exact-on-tissue estimator is WORSE THAN PREDICTING NOTHING by the official aggregate metric. classical_sum_to_one recovers tissue pixels to RMSE 1.29e-6 (near machine precision, as theory predicts for noiseless data) yet scores s1 = 1.179, far worse than the no-training zero_prediction reference (s1 = 0.430), because the 5-9% boundary band carries RMSE 3.84 with individual values up to 11.5 volume fraction (grossly unphysical). Simplex projection clips those outliers and wins (s1 = 0.0508) WITHOUT improving the model: its tissue RMSE is the same 1.2e-6. CONSEQUENCE: a pooled RMSE over a two-regime phantom is dominated by the smallest, most mis-modelled population, so reporting s1 alone would rank a clipped estimator above an exact one for the wrong reason. Regime-separated reporting is mandatory for this benchmark. Caveat: synthetic data only; boundary-band fraction and geometry differ from real AAPM data.

## Statement

On SYNTHETIC AAPM-schema data (EXP-003/RUN-0003, 17 val cases, 32x32, noiseless), an exact-on-tissue estimator is WORSE THAN PREDICTING NOTHING by the official aggregate metric. classical_sum_to_one recovers tissue pixels to RMSE 1.29e-6 (near machine precision, as theory predicts for noiseless data) yet scores s1 = 1.179, far worse than the no-training zero_prediction reference (s1 = 0.430), because the 5-9% boundary band carries RMSE 3.84 with individual values up to 11.5 volume fraction (grossly unphysical). Simplex projection clips those outliers and wins (s1 = 0.0508) WITHOUT improving the model: its tissue RMSE is the same 1.2e-6. CONSEQUENCE: a pooled RMSE over a two-regime phantom is dominated by the smallest, most mis-modelled population, so reporting s1 alone would rank a clipped estimator above an exact one for the wrong reason. Regime-separated reporting is mandatory for this benchmark. Caveat: synthetic data only; boundary-band fraction and geometry differ from real AAPM data.
