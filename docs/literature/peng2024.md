## Problem
Image-domain DECT decomposition by matrix inversion is badly conditioned (spectral overlap, correlated basis
attenuation) and amplifies noise. TV-type iterative priors blur/alter noise texture and are slow; supervised DL needs
paired GT material maps unavailable clinically; a prior unsupervised data-fidelity-only method (their ref 24) needs
per-case optimization and early stopping.

## Method (Sec. 2)
- Image-domain linear model (Sec. 2.1): per-pixel LAC at high/low energy = linear combination of two basis volume
  fractions; composition matrix from mean values of two manually chosen uniform ROIs; direct inversion = matrix inverse.
  No polychromatic/projection model (beam hardening ignored, acknowledged in Sec. 5).
- Inputs: concatenated high/low-energy CT images. Outputs: two basis-material (volume fraction) images, single pass.
- Framework (Sec. 2.2, Fig. 1): GAN. Generator G does decomposition; loss = least-squares data fidelity between
  acquired DECT and DECT re-synthesised from G's material maps via the composition matrix, plus adversarial loss
  (weighted sum, Eq. 9; weight lambda user-defined). Discriminator D separates low-noise reference material images
  from G's high-noise outputs; reference images need not be paired with inputs. Architectures and hyperparameters are
  in the supplement (UNVERIFIED).
- Data (Sec. 2.3): (a) physical phantom: Gammex with iodine inserts 15/10/5 mg/mL, Varian Edge onboard CBCT at
  140/100 kVp, 100/50/25 mAs, FDK; trained on 1500 SIMULATED phantom images (random polygons, iodine 1-15 mg/mL,
  excluding 5/10/15). (b) Simulation: head from CQ500 (1426 train slices/18 pts, 500 test/9 pts) and lung from TCIA
  LungCT-Diagnosis (1766 train/23 pts, 500 test/11 pts); GT bone/soft-tissue maps made from SECT by threshold and
  normalisation, DECT forward-simulated (details in supplement). Reference low-noise images from separate patients
  (12 brain, 15 lung; 1000 slices each), augmented by rotation/translation. (c) Clinical: head and lung patients, bone-
  tissue and bone-iodine separation; scanner appears to be TwinBeam (Au/Sn filtered spectra) per Sec. 5.
- Baselines: direct matrix inversion, TV-regularised iterative decomposition. Metrics: ROI SD, MSE, SSIM, electron density.

## Reported results
- Abstract/Sec. 3.2, Table II: SD reduced 97% (head) and 91% (lung) vs direct inversion in simulations; SSIM > 0.95
  vs GT. Table II ROI mean+-SD, e.g. brain slice (a): noisy inversion 0.97+-0.43, TV 0.91+-0.03, proposed 0.99+-0.01.
- Sec. 3.1, Table I (iodine, mg/mL, mean+-SD): full dose ROI1 15.01+-6.94 (inversion), 13.53+-0.49 (TV), 15.02+-0.03
  (proposed); ROI2 proposed 9.91+-0.21 (ref 10); ROI3 5.15+-0.15 (ref 5). Quarter dose ROI3 proposed 5.33+-0.24
  vs inversion 6.10+-12.94. Proposed most accurate and lowest noise per authors.
- Sec. 3.3 clinical: SD reduced > 95% (brain) and > 93% (lung) vs inversion (ROI tables are in supplement, Tables S.4-S.5).
- Authors state TV lowers means (L1 shrinkage) while proposed preserves means.

## Limits
- Authors (Sec. 5): image domain so beam hardening not corrected (noiseless simulations still have errors); basis
  materials fixed at training (retrain for different bases); weak low-contrast soft-tissue differentiation with
  TwinBeam's small spectral separation; bone-iodine separation evaluated only on a patient (no quantitative phantom).
- Reader notes: simulated GT derive from thresholded SECT (crude 2-material truth); no clinical GT exists; evaluation
  is mostly ROI noise reduction, which the GAN prior can inflate by smoothing; no adversarial-hallucination analysis
  seen in the main text. Composition matrix from ROIs implies a scanner/protocol-specific calibration.

## Relevance to CTsynth
- Complements Hellwege: image-domain, simple linear forward model, plus a learned noise prior from unpaired references.
  Useful as a baseline and as an example of the data a virtual-patient population must supply: paired DECT images with
  known two-material maps (their simulated GT) and noise realisations at several dose levels.
- Their phantom-trained-on-synthetic-polygons strategy (random shapes, held-out iodine concentrations) is a precedent
  for procedurally generated virtual phantoms with held-out concentration validation.
- Shows the cost of an inverse-crime-free test: sim GT via SECT thresholding and a linear model is not a physics-grounded
  polychromatic truth; CTsynth could improve this with explicit spectra and tissue composition.
- Iodine quantification Table I gives a concrete accuracy/noise benchmark per dose.
